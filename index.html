One shop.
Many games.

A recognisable Flagship frame, with room for every game to be itself.

Use this reference to make shop communications and digital experiences feel related, from a Friday draft poster to a busy afternoon at the counter.

Keep the frame.

The boat, paper, ink, type and information hierarchy give Flagship a dependable signature.

Change the atmosphere.

Game imagery, a supporting colour and the campaign headline can bring a different world into that frame.

What the existing materials teach us

Keep

The memorable gold boat, direct event titles, confident scale and sense of a real playing community. The strongest pieces already separate the game world from a calm information area.

Improve

Competing type styles, outlined text over busy artwork, changing logo placement and small event details make the shop harder to recognise. Give each message a clear first, second and third read.

A small boat.
A clear signature.

Keep the original mark intact. Recognition comes from using it consistently, rather than changing it to suit each game.

Original supplied mark
Same asset. Different ground.

Give it room

Proposed clear space: one quarter of the displayed image width on every side. At a 96px image width, keep at least 24px free around it. Start at 64px image width for digital use; check the actual display.

The PNG has a square transparent canvas. Preserve that canvas and aspect ratio. The download is the original 480 × 480 file.

Keep it whole

  • Use the supplied gold. No colour filters or recolouring.
  • No stretching, rotation, tracing or recreated geometry.
  • Place it on a quiet surface, outside the artwork.
  • Set “Flagship Games” as companion text. The supplied mark contains no official wordmark.

and a little brass.

A warm, practical core palette leaves space for colourful game worlds. Click a swatch to copy its value.

Brass is the proposed interface colour. The logo’s sampled raster gold is #BAAC7F; that sample is not a print-colour specification.

Use colour by role

Paper is the ground. Sheet holds readable content. Ink carries text and primary actions. Brass adds emphasis and connects to the boat.

Use a quieter line for decorative divisions and a stronger boundary for controls. Never make white small text sit on brass.

Paper is a structure,
not an effect.

Think in sheets, image windows and generous margins. A shared rhythm does more than adding a folded corner to everything.

Information sheet

Build from a shared rhythm

4px base. Use 8px within a small group, 16–24px within a component, and 32–48px between groups. Let content determine the height.

Sharp sheets and softly eased controls belong together. Reserve a quiet fold for an editorial or campaign sheet. Inputs and status labels stay straightforward.

Density follows the task

Public pages can breathe. A catalogue needs comparable image windows. A counter needs compact rows and visible totals. Carry the same foundations through each.

On a narrow screen, stack the content in reading order. Keep primary touch actions at least 44px tall and give long titles room to wrap.

What stays fixed

  • Unchanged mark and consistent signature.
  • Core typography for dates, prices and conditions.
  • A calm, readable information area.
  • One obvious next action.

What can change

  • Rights-cleared game imagery and its crop.
  • One supporting campaign colour.
  • Headline scale and image-to-information ratio.
  • Square, portrait or wide format.

Quiet, explicit states

In stockPayment pendingPayment failedOut of stock

Status labels are not buttons. Selection, success and campaign colour serve different jobs.

Keyboard and touch. Visible 3px focus outlines with space around them. Keep controls large enough to use, and avoid hover-only information.

Loading and recovery. Keep the pending state visible, prevent duplicate submission, and explain how to check or retry. A toast never substitutes for the record itself.

Speak like a good
person at the counter.

Friendly, knowledgeable and direct. Welcome new players without talking down to experienced ones.

Instead of

“Dominate the ultimate epic showdown!”

Say

“Bring your deck. We’ll help you find a table.”

Instead of

“Transaction unsuccessful.”

Say

“Payment wasn’t confirmed. Check the status before trying again.”

Instead of

“You’re all set!”

Say

“Your place is reserved. Payment is still pending.”

Lead with what someone needs to know: what it is, when it happens, what it costs and what to do next. Keep specialist game terms where they help, and explain unfamiliar formats.
